\section*{Context}
The capstone artifact is not a generic classifier. It is a
threat-aware quantum-network controller that selects routes and allocates
limited qubits while link quality, replay capacity, allocator behavior, and
adversarial regimes change~\cite{suresh2021,mehrabi2021}. Inherited
graduate-assistant (GA) routing work already evaluates aggregate efficiency,
regret, robustness, and stability. DSCI~602 adds a distinct question: whether
those aggregates hide unequal service across competing flows or service
classes.

This is a fairness problem because the system influences a scarce-resource
decision---who gets a successful, timely, and qubit-proportionate path when
capacity and context are constrained. The stakeholders are the flows (or
planned service classes) competing for entanglement routing, plus any
downstream users of those paths. The relevant data are not demographic
records. They are run traces: group/service-class identifiers, demand,
selected action/route, allocated qubits, fidelity, retries or starvation,
latency, success, and context quality. DSCI~601 already implemented a
post-run fairness sidecar (\texttt{RunSpec}, \texttt{OutcomeRecord},
\texttt{QuantumFairnessAdapter}, EQUITAS audit/mitigation fields) and a
\texttt{QuantumEquitasMediator} class seam with a standalone test. Live
attachment to the quantum runner is not complete; quantum-specific equity
experiments remain planned. The analysis below is therefore prospective and
must not be read as a completed fairness result.

Why the dimension is at stake is already visible in inherited, configuration-
specific routing evidence. For ThompsonSampling with Tb replay under the
OnlineAdaptive threat, Oracle-normalized efficiency falls from 89.2\% at
$1\times$ replay to 84.9\% at $1.5\times$, then recovers to 90.8\% at
$2\times$. That non-monotonic interaction is an aggregate finding. The
fairness risk is that the loss at $1.5\times$, or the recovery at $2\times$,
is not distributed evenly: some classes may absorb retries, starvation, or
latency while others retain reliable service~\cite{obermeyer2019,buolamwini2018}.

\section*{Risk Analysis}
Suresh and Guttag's life-cycle model is the right vocabulary for this
pipeline~\cite{suresh2021}. Bias can enter before any fairness score is
applied.

\paragraph{Representation and grouping.}
If service classes are undefined, or if demand is unnormalized, the system
cannot distinguish unequal access from unequal need. The current post-run
record supports group, success, latency, and reward. It does not yet carry
demand, allocated qubits, selected route, fidelity, retries/starvation, or
context quality. Measuring only success on incomplete denominators is a
classic measurement harm: the audit can look fair while starvation is
invisible~\cite{mehrabi2021,barocas2023}.

\paragraph{Historical and aggregation bias.}
Bandit policies trained on aggregate reward will prefer historically
successful routes. Under escalating threats, that preference can lock in a
winner-take-routes pattern: high-context or high-degree flows keep being
served because they previously succeeded, while low-context flows become
retry sinks. Obermeyer et al.\ showed how an ostensibly race-neutral
objective (cost) produced racial disparity because the proxy was
unequal~\cite{obermeyer2019}. The analogous proxy here is aggregate
Oracle-normalized efficiency. Optimizing it can ratify unequal service.

\paragraph{Learning and evaluation bias.}
Context quality is itself a fairness variable. RQ2 of the capstone asks how
disparities change as disruptions become structured (Markov) or
online-adaptive, especially when context quality differs. If one class
systematically receives poorer context, a contextual bandit can appear
robust in the mean while concentrating failures on that class. Gender Shades
documented error-rate gaps that aggregate accuracy concealed~\cite{buolamwini2018}.
The same concealment is plausible when only network-wide efficiency is
reported.

\paragraph{Choice of fairness definition.}
Demographic parity (equal success rates), equalized odds (equal error
structure), and calibration (scores mean the same thing across classes) are
not simultaneously satisfiable in general~\cite{hardt2016,chouldechova2017,kleinberg2017}.
For quantum routing, equalized access to successful paths under comparable
demand is closer to the operational goal than calibration of a clinical
risk score. Forcing a single definition as a hard constraint can destroy
utility; ignoring definitional conflict is ``fairness through unawareness''
with extra steps~\cite{barocas2023,hardt2016}. The scientifically honest
object is therefore a utility--disparity frontier, not a claim that one
$\lambda$ makes the controller fair.

\section*{Mitigations}
The mitigations below are already the DSCI~602 deliverable spine. They are
design changes, not slogans.

\paragraph{Specify groups and denominators before mediation (D1).}
Define operational service classes and demand normalization; add schema
fields for allocated qubits, selected route, fidelity, retries/starvation,
and context quality; test equal-service, unequal-service, zero-demand, and
missing-context fixtures. This follows the measurement discipline in the
fairness literature: name the group, name the outcome, name the
denominator~\cite{suresh2021,mehrabi2021,barocas2023}.

\paragraph{Attach a live mediator with an off-switch (D2).}
The existing \texttt{QuantumEquitasMediator} seam should be attached at a
documented policy-score or allocator boundary, implementing
\[
S^{\mathrm{fair}}_t(a)=U_t(a)-\lambda\max(0,\widehat{\Delta}_t(a)-\varepsilon)-\gamma C_t(a),
\]
where $U_t$ is utility, $\widehat{\Delta}_t$ is an estimated disparity,
$\varepsilon$ is a tolerated slack, $C_t$ is a context-quality penalty, and
$\lambda=\gamma=0$ is the identity (no-mediation) baseline. Unit tests must
show identity, threshold, monotonicity, and determinism. This is in-processing
mitigation in Mehrabi et al.'s taxonomy, not post-hoc reporting~\cite{mehrabi2021,hardt2016}.

\paragraph{Evaluate jointly, not sequentially (D3--D4).}
Screen a small matrix, lock a manifest, then estimate main effects and
interactions of mediation $\times$ threat $\times$ policy $\times$ allocator
$\times$ capacity. Report paired differences against the unmediated baseline
with seed-level uncertainty. When aggregate efficiency and class-level
starvation disagree, inspect mechanisms rather than averaging them away.
That is the lesson of both Gender Shades and the Obermeyer cost-proxy
failure~\cite{buolamwini2018,obermeyer2019,kleinberg2017}.

\paragraph{Document residual risk.}
Even a well-tuned mediator cannot satisfy every formal criterion at
once~\cite{kleinberg2017,chouldechova2017}. Context-quality gaps may remain
if the environment does not emit them. The seam may attach in the wrong
place (policy vs.\ allocator). Live monitoring is currently unsupported; only
post-run audit exists. These are residual risks, not future-work decoration.

\section*{Limitations}
This analysis does not claim completed fairness-mediated quantum runs, a live
attached mediator, advisor approval of service classes, or clinical
deployment. Clinical fairness remains a secondary/future validation path.
GA routing results are inherited infrastructure, not DSCI~602 equity
findings. The 89.2/84.9/90.8 series is one validated
ThompsonSampling+Tb/OnlineAdaptive slice, not a universal law of replay
capacity. Group definitions are still an advisor checkpoint. Until D1
freezes classes and denominators, any disparity number would be
under-specified.

Treated as a venue-style broader-impacts / limitations note, the responsible
claim is narrow: quantum-network controllers can produce service inequity
even when they look efficient; the appropriate response is to measure
flow-conditioned outcomes, mediate with an auditable off-switch, and report
the utility--disparity frontier rather than a single fairness
score~\cite{barocas2023,hardt2016,suresh2021}.
